# The Statewide Question — Mandatory Procedure, Judicial Discretion, and Constitutional Limits

*Public commentary, October 18, 2026. Not a court filing or service of papers. The factual allegations below are Rosario's contentions; the identified reporter's-transcript quotations are taken from the supplied QUESTION(9).md and should be checked against the certified record before formal filing.*

## Question presented

> **May a trial judge invoke judicial discretion, substantive correctness, or harmlessness to excuse noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 614's mandatory procedure for communicating legal information to a deliberating jury, when the alleged departure also implicates the litigant's Fourteenth Amendment right to due process?**

**Controlling proposition:** A judge cannot convert a mandatory statutory obligation into a discretionary choice merely by asserting judicial authority.

This argument concerns **how the judge delivered information to the jury**, not whether his legal language was correct. The first inquiry is whether the prescribed procedure preceded transmission. Arguments about correctness, waiver, and prejudice do not themselves establish that it did.

## 1. Constitutional and statutory hierarchy

**Fourteenth Amendment — Due process.** A state judge's discretionary authority is limited by federal constitutional due process. An undisclosed judicial communication that meaningfully deprives a party of an opportunity to address the law sent to a deliberating jury may raise a distinct federal due-process concern. Not every violation of state procedural law automatically violates the Fourteenth Amendment.

**California Constitution, article I, section 16 — Civil jury trial.** California independently protects the state-court civil jury trial. Its institutional integrity reinforces the significance of the court's compliance with rules governing jury communication.

**Code of Civil Procedure section 614 — Mandatory method.** When a deliberating jury requests legal information, the statute requires that the information be given **“in the presence of, or after notice to, the parties or counsel.”** A judge has no discretion to replace that command with a personal finding that the answer is useful, accurate, or harmless.

**Seventh Amendment — Important but limited federal analogy.** The federal civil-jury guarantee does **not presently apply to state-court trials** under *Minneapolis & St. Louis R. Co. v. Bombolis* (1916) 241 U.S. 211. Justice Gorsuch questioned that doctrine in his statement respecting denial of certiorari in *Thomas v. Humboldt County* (Oct. 14, 2025), but the Supreme Court did **not** overturn it. The Seventh Amendment should therefore not be pleaded as an already-incorporated independent right in this California state action.

**Other amendments.** The Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause directly restrains the federal government; the Fourteenth Amendment supplies the relevant federal due-process protection against state action. The Sixth Amendment's criminal-jury guarantees do not directly govern this civil case. The First Amendment does not supply a jury-communication claim merely because Rosario objected; any petitioning or retaliation claim would need its own facts.

The Supreme Court of California stated in *Nelson v. Southern Pacific Co.* (1937) 8 Cal.2d 648, 655, that unauthorized post-retirement judge–jury communication contrary to section 614 is a serious irregularity affecting the substance of the jury-trial right. **Inference A:** the statutory manner of delivery protects the process itself, independently of the legal merit of the instruction.

## 2. One historical question must be answered first

Rosario alleges that Judge Garrett L. Wong transmitted a **materially enhanced Reply No. 2** to the deliberating jury **before Rosario or his legal-support team saw the completed enhancement and had an opportunity to object to its delivery**. He says the team witnessed the sequence firsthand. Their observations are presently identified as **potential corroborating evidence**, not sworn findings.

The precise statutory question is whether the information was given **in the presence of, or after notice to, the parties or counsel**. Rosario's further interpretation is that when a draft is materially changed, the notice must cover the change in time to allow meaningful participation. Section 614 does not expressly require *party approval* of the final text.

To decide that question, identify **(a)** the version actually sent, **(b)** the version actually shown to the parties, **(c)** when and how each was distributed, and **(d)** the moment the jury received the completed response.

**Inference B:** discussing an earlier draft does not, without evidence connecting the versions, establish notice of a subsequently added material term. But that gap alone is not conclusive proof of non-notice.

## 3. The court expressly claimed prior presentation for Reply No. 1

Judge Wong stated:

> “The Court presented that response in Jury request number one to each party before delivering it to the Jury.”  
> **11 RT 6:19–21.**

**Inference C:** This is a specific claim concerning **Reply No. 1**. It does not, without an additional showing, establish advance disclosure of the later-discussed highway/street enhancement in **Reply No. 2**. A reader must not silently transfer the judge's claim from one response to the other.

## 4. Rosario identified the enhancement and said it had already been sent

Rosario responded:

> “Inaccurate, Your Honor. You enhanced it by adding that highways also indicates street. That is not in the actual black-letter law. You enhanced the statement and you sent that back. That is a direct influence over the Jury.”  
> **11 RT 8:7–12.**

Judge Wong's immediate answer was:

> “Sir, do you want to take a look at this code section? It says, ‘Highway includes street’, close quote.”  
> **11 RT 8:13–16.**

**Inference D:** The accusation named an **enhancement already transmitted**. The judge's answer defended **accuracy of wording**. It identified no time, person, or method of pre-transmission notice of the completed Reply No. 2 enhancement. Accordingly, that answer **does not prove statutory compliance**.

**Inference E:** Because the judge had a direct opportunity to describe prior delivery, his decision instead to defend wording permits Rosario to argue an **inference of justification in place of factual refutation**. It is **not a binding admission** that there was no notice. The transcript does not show the judge expressly agreeing that Rosario first saw the completed version after the jury did.

## 5. Rosario disputed the court's account and requested reconstruction

When the judge described the earlier objection history, Rosario said:

> “That wasn't our understanding.”  
> **11 RT 10:9–12.**

Rosario then referred to objections made outside the reporter's presence and stated:

> “I would like to either reconstruct the record or we file my objection for that.”  
> **11 RT 10:23–11:13.**

The court also asserted acquiescence or lack of earlier objection, **11 RT 10:14–19**.

**Inference F:** The record establishes a **contemporaneous dispute over the court's description of the prior process**, not Rosario's silent acceptance of that description. The request for reconstruction explains why the unreported chronology requires evidentiary clarification. It does not independently establish whose recollection is right.

## 6. The judge's invocation of discretion did not identify a delivery event

Judge Wong stated:

> “Your objections are noted. They are overruled and the Court believes that this is within its discretion to issue these responses in order to clarify for the Jury some issues that they have raised by way of their requests.”  
> **11 RT 11:28–12:4.**

The judge also described the supplied definition as nonprejudicial (**11 RT 7:27–8:6**, **before** Rosario's specific accusation at 8:7–12). Rosario renewed his complaint about **“you sending back enhancements”** (**11 RT 13:16–22**); the judge replied that the section 657 remedy was premature (**11 RT 13:23–25**).

**Inference G:** **Discretion to issue** answers a different question from **compliance in delivery**. The former cannot stand in for the latter.

**Inference H:** **Accuracy and harmlessness** do not establish that notice took place. Harmlessness may be relevant **after a violation is found** to the question of remedy, but it cannot retroactively make a missing procedural event occur.

**Inference I:** The later procedural response to the requested remedy likewise did not identify who presented the final enhancement, at what time, or how. This continues the recorded pattern of **answering propriety, authority, or remedy rather than substantiating chronology**.

## 7. The unproduced staff corroboration and timestamp trail

Rosario reports that his legal-support team observed the completed messages only **after** their transmission to the jury and can recount the sequence from personal knowledge. To turn that account into evidence, each person should separately identify exactly what they saw, when, and how they independently knew transmission had already occurred; a California-compliant declaration under penalty of perjury can memorialize those observations.

The response and delivery were managed through the court. Potential documentary sources include original jury request forms, versions of the replies, time-stamped clerk records, electronic sending logs, emails, docket notations, or any other contemporaneous distribution record. **The quoted exchanges do not show Judge Wong asking court staff to corroborate advance notice or producing a timestamp or transmission log to rebut Rosario's allegation.**

**Inference J — Directly established by the quoted exchange:** During the cited discussion, **no staff witness or timestamped record was identified to corroborate pre-delivery notice of the completed Reply No. 2 enhancement**. This describes the *hearing record*, not the universe of records that may exist.

**Inference K — Reasonable circumstantial argument:** A record showing precisely when the final response was provided to the parties and jury could materially resolve the accusation. When confronted with a specific challenge, the judge responded with accuracy and discretion rather than referencing such a record. Rosario may argue this **failure to substantiate compliance when challenged strengthens the inference that the claimed procedural step did not occur**, particularly if independent eyewitnesses consistently corroborate the chronology.

**Inference L — Necessary evidentiary limit:** The judge was not automatically required to summon court staff, generate a log, or conduct an evidentiary hearing merely because Rosario accused him. The absence of a produced log **does not prove** a log existed, was suppressed, showed misconduct, or was legally required to have been created. Neither the uncalled court staff nor the proposed party-side witnesses have testified under oath in the cited exchange. **The inference becomes stronger only with authenticated version/timing records or firsthand testimony**.

**Tacit-acknowledgment theory, accurately stated:** The judge **did not directly refute the specific delivery chronology in these excerpts**, but instead defended his authority and the legal content. This is **non-refutation by justification**, a circumstance from which Rosario draws an inference. It is **not a conclusive judicial admission or an independently proved Fourteenth Amendment violation**.

## 8. What the reviewing court should decide

The appellate court should separate four questions in sequence.

1. **Meaning of section 614:** Does its mandatory presence-or-notice requirement extend to a material revision in the information ultimately transmitted?
2. **Historical compliance:** What completed response did the jury receive, and what legally sufficient presence or notice was afforded beforehand? What do the court-controlled records and firsthand witnesses establish?
3. **Constitutional significance:** If that procedure was bypassed, did the circumstances also deprive Rosario of a meaningful opportunity to be heard under the Fourteenth Amendment or impair California's civil jury-trial guarantee?
4. **Consequences:** What preservation, waiver, prejudice, and remedy standards apply *after* the statutory and factual questions have been addressed?

An assertion of waiver does not prove what was delivered or when. A finding that the legal wording was accurate does not prove notice. A harmlessness analysis may affect the consequence of error but cannot establish that section 614 was obeyed.

## Conclusion

> **A judge cannot convert a mandatory statutory obligation into a discretionary choice merely by asserting judicial authority.** Section 614 regulates the **delivery** of requested legal information to a deliberating jury. Rosario accuses the trial court of delivering a materially enhanced response without first affording the legally required notice and opportunity. In the recorded confrontation, the court defended **accuracy, absence of prejudice, and discretion**, while the excerpts identify no verified advance delivery of the completed Reply No. 2 enhancement and no court-staff or timestamp corroboration. The appropriate judicial inquiry is whether the statutory procedure actually occurred—not whether the judge had good reasons for the words he sent.
