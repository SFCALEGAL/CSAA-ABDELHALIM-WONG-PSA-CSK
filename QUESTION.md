# The Statewide Question: Mandatory Procedure and Constitutional Due Process

*Public commentary — October 18, 2026. Not a court filing or service of process. Transcript quotations and pinpoints follow the source document; allegations and interpretive inferences are attributed to Rosario.*

## Question Presented

> **May a trial judge invoke judicial discretion, substantive correctness, or harmlessness to excuse noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 614’s mandatory procedure for communicating legal information to a deliberating jury, when the alleged departure also implicates the litigant’s Fourteenth Amendment right to due process?**

**Controlling thesis: A judge cannot convert a mandatory statutory obligation into a discretionary choice merely by asserting judicial authority.**

The critical event is **delivery**. Rosario alleges that Judge Garrett L. Wong transmitted materially enhanced legal information to the deliberating jury before Rosario and the witnesses on the Rosario legal team were given an opportunity to review and object to the completed enhancement. The judge responded by defending what the law said and his authority to issue the responses. The resulting statewide question is whether a claim of authority can stand in place of compliance with the statutory method of communication.

## Constitutional chain

**United States Constitution, Fourteenth Amendment — Due Process.** State judicial power operates within federal constitutional limits. Rosario's claimed deprivation is loss of meaningful participation before the court's authoritative legal response entered deliberations. His constitutional theory attaches to the **timing and method of transmission**.

**California Constitution, article I, section 16 — Civil jury trial.** California’s jury-trial protection adds a state constitutional reason to protect deliberations from judicial communications outside the prescribed process.

**Code of Civil Procedure section 614 — Delivery requirement.** When deliberating jurors request information on a point of law, the information **“must be given in the presence of, or after notice to, the parties or counsel.”** The command governs the judge’s method of providing the information. Rosario argues that meaningful notice of materially modified information must address the completed modification before it is given to jurors.

**California Supreme Court — *Nelson v. Southern Pacific Co.* (1937) 8 Cal.2d 648, 655.** *Nelson* recognized the significance of unauthorized judicial communication with a deliberating jury under section 614 for the substance of the jury-trial right. The procedural requirement protects institutional integrity rather than merely regulating word choice.

**Constitutional conclusion advanced.** Where the statutory procedure is bypassed and the party loses a constitutionally meaningful opportunity to address authoritative judicial information entering deliberations, the same act supports a Fourteenth Amendment due-process challenge. The federal constitutional ground is a distinct inquiry rooted in the alleged deprivation of meaningful process.

## The accusation and the witnesses

Rosario and **multiple witnesses on the Rosario legal team** identify the same disputed delivery sequence: the completed, enhanced judicial communication reached the jury before they received the opportunity to examine it. Rosario raised the accusation in the judge’s presence, and the record shows his effort to reconstruct events outside the reporter’s presence.

Courtroom personnel also included the **bailiff, court reporter, and clerks**. In the cited confrontation, the judge identified no corroborating statement from these court personnel and brought forward no staff witness, distribution timestamp, or transmission log establishing prior disclosure of the completed Reply No. 2 enhancement. Rosario advances this absence of contemporaneous corroboration, in conjunction with his immediate objection and the Rosario legal-team witnesses, as circumstantial support for the claimed chronology.

**The witnesses did not need to recite “section 614” or “Fourteenth Amendment” to describe the challenged conduct.** The factual accusation was that the court **enhanced the communication, sent it to the jury, and left Rosario without prior review of the completed enhancement**. Section 614 and due process give that factual charge its legal significance.

## The judge’s recorded response to the accusation

Judge Wong expressly stated with respect to **Reply No. 1**:

> “The Court presented that response in Jury request number one to each party before delivering it to the Jury.” — **11 RT 6:19–21**

Rosario’s specific confrontation concerned an enhancement to **Reply No. 2**:

> “Inaccurate, Your Honor. You enhanced it by adding that highways also indicates street. That is not in the actual black-letter law. You enhanced the statement and you sent that back. That is a direct influence over the Jury.” — **11 RT 8:7–12**

The judge immediately answered:

> “Sir, do you want to take a look at this code section? It says, ‘Highway includes street’, close quote.” — **11 RT 8:13–16**

Later the judge stated:

> “Your objections are noted. They are overruled and the Court believes that this is within its discretion to issue these responses in order to clarify for the Jury some issues that they have raised by way of their requests.” — **11 RT 11:28–12:4**

**Correlated inference — what the judge addressed.** The first statement expressly addressed presentation of No. 1. The accusation identified the enhanced No. 2 communication as already sent. The immediate answer defended statutory wording; the later answer defended judicial discretion to issue the replies. Rosario characterizes that pattern as **justification of the challenged act in place of an account of the statutory delivery step**.

**Correlated inference — tacit acknowledgment through justification.** Rosario argues that the judge treated the act of adding and sending information as within his authority while responding to a charge whose operative feature was sending it **before party review**. The inference rests on what the judge affirmatively defended, the contemporaneous accusation, the disputed chronology, and the availability of courtroom witnesses and transmission records. It is an inference Rosario asks the reader to draw from the full exchange.

**Correlated inference — the personnel and records.** The quoted exchange identifies no bailiff, reporter, clerk, timestamped log, or contemporaneous delivery event verifying prior presentation of the materially completed Reply No. 2. Rosario treats the absence of an identified corroborating event during the judicial confrontation as a salient feature of the record.

## Proof chain: transcript → procedure → constitutional principle

**Step 1 — The statutory event.** The jury was deliberating and received legal information from the judge. Section 614 governs the manner of that communication.

**Step 2 — The charged delivery sequence.** Rosario specifically accused the judge of enhancing and sending the completed legal response. Rosario and multiple witnesses on the Rosario legal team identify receipt by the parties after delivery to the jury.

**Step 3 — The judge’s opportunity to answer.** Rosario challenged the event face-to-face, disputed the judge's earlier account, and asked to reconstruct the unreported record. The judge had occasion to identify the date, time, person, or record of advance notice.

**Step 4 — The answer actually recorded.** The judge defended the correctness of the added language, described his discretion to issue responses, and rejected relief. The discussion identifies no court-staff corroboration or timestamp establishing prior notice of the completed No. 2 enhancement.

**Step 5 — Evidentiary inference.** Rosario asks the reviewing court to infer that the judge’s substantive and discretionary justifications functioned as a response to the accusation while leaving the procedural allegation unrebutted in the recorded exchange. The presence of multiple Rosario legal-team witnesses and courtroom personnel makes the chronology an identifiable factual issue with concrete potential sources of corroboration.

**Step 6 — Statutory principle.** The court's discretion to decide and explain the law remains subject to the mandatory presence-or-notice procedure. Accuracy of the legal content is distinct from the manner of its delivery.

**Step 7 — Constitutional significance.** If the court's communication deprived Rosario of a meaningful opportunity to address a material legal change before it became authoritative guidance inside the jury room, the delivery event also implicates procedural due process under the Fourteenth Amendment and the integrity of California's civil jury-trial guarantee.

**Step 8 — Judicial accountability.** The reviewing court should identify the actual statutory notice event and adjudicate the disputed chronology. Arguments concerning the wisdom of the instruction, waiver, and remedy follow the determination of whether the mandatory delivery procedure was complied with.

## Proposed statewide rule

> **A judge cannot convert a mandatory statutory obligation into a discretionary choice merely by asserting judicial authority. When the deliberating jury requests legal information, compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 614 must be determined by the manner and timing of communication to the jury. Judicial discretion to formulate and issue legal information cannot replace the Legislature's required procedure; where that departure also denies meaningful participation, the Fourteenth Amendment imposes an additional constitutional constraint.**

The accusation is specific, and the recorded responses are specific. **Rosario alleged delivery before party review. The judge defended the content and his authority to send it. The resulting question is compliance with the required delivery procedure.**
