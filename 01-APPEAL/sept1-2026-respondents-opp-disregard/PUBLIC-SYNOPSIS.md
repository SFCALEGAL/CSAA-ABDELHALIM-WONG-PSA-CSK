## Theories already on the face of this opposition

The following points appear in the received paper. This page does not add a theory that is not on that face.

1. **Record disagreement is not a strike ground.** Appellant and respondent disagree about the content of the appellate record. The opposition says that disagreement is not grounds to disregard respondent's argument because appellant disagrees with the implications of statements on the record.

2. **Preservation and waiver.** Respondent's point, as stated, is that the lack of record on several key points is the point: appellant failed to preserve and object, on the record, to the failure to give certain instructions (CACI 705, 707, or 710). Proposing jury instructions, the opposition says, is not the same as objecting when they are not given. If a discussion about which instructions would be given occurred, the opposition says it is not in the appellate record and that this is waiver of the alleged instructional error.

3. **Code of Civil Procedure section 647.** The opposition quotes that a refusal to give a jury instruction is deemed excepted to, then states there is no evidence in the appellate record that the trial court refused to give CACI 705, 707, or 710. Proposed instructions are not, on this paper, treated as proof of a refusal.

4. **Packet volume.** The opposition states appellant proposed 152 jury instructions, many duplicative, and the court gave 23. It says the record does not show how the instructions were selected or that the court refused any, as opposed to the parties agreeing. It points to Reporter's Transcript volume 11 (RT 11) discussions of juror questions and says appellant never raised a prior refusal of his proposed instructions.

5. **Waiver and invited error authorities cited on the face.** The opposition cites *Telles Transport, Inc. v. Workers' Comp. Appeals Bd.* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 1159, 1167 (waiver), and *Norgart v. Upjohn Co.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 383, 403, with *Huffman v. Interstate Brands Corp.* (cited as *Huffman*, 121 Cal.App.4th at p. 706) (invited error).

6. **Invited-error reading of RT 11.** The opposition says appellant's on-record conduct invited the instructional claim: appellant objected to the jury considering the Vehicle Code sections at issue and moved for a mistrial, including the quoted statement that the jury should not be "requesting Vehicle Code sections that were not presented during the trial in view of the evidence" (RT 11, 4:18-25). The opposition treats that as evidence appellant did not request those Vehicle Code instructions at trial.

7. **Seven challenged passages.** The opposition answers seven portions of Respondent's Opening Brief that the August 20 motion challenged (pages 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, and 14 as cited). Across those answers the paper says: some sentences are argument rather than a fact needing a pin cite; other sentences do cite RT 11; a conclusion section is "pure argument"; and pointing out that evidence is absent from the record is not a CRC 8.204(a)(1)(C) violation. It quotes RT 11, 4:8-10, 4:15-28, 5:28-6:6, and 6:16-21, including appellant's statement agreeing that the jury "may not consider" the Vehicle Code matter "because it is not part of the instructions," and the court's statement that appellant did not object to the language submitted on Jury Request No. 1.

8. **Relief line.** "Appellant's motion seeks to disregard portions of Respondent's brief because he does not agree with them, not because they violate any rule or law. Respondent requests the Court deny Appellant's motion in its entirety."
