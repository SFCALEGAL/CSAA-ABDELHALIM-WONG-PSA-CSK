*Contra Costa Proposed legal argument*
Moradi-Shalal Preserves This Action Because the Core Wrong Is Independent
Common-Law Deceit, Not a Private Claim for Unfair Settlement Practices
Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Insurance Companies (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287
holds that Insurance Code section 790.03 does not create an implied private
cause of action for a third-party claimant. It does not immunize insurers
from traditional tort liability. The Supreme Court expressly stated:

*"[A]part from administrative remedies, the courts retain jurisdiction to
impose civil damages or other remedies against insurers in appropriate
common law actions, based on such traditional theories as fraud."*

(Id. at pp. 304-305.) The Court further recognized that punitive damages
may remain available where fraud, oppression, or malice is proved. (Id. at
p. 305; Civ. Code, sec. 3294.)

Plaintiff's claim falls directly within that reservation. Plaintiff does
not seek damages for a violation of Insurance Code section 790.03, does not
assert third-party insurance bad faith, does not allege breach of an
insurance contract, and does not seek policy benefits or the value of the
underlying bodily-injury claim. Plaintiff alleges the traditional tort of
intentional deceit under Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710, subdivision (1).

The core wrong is a false representation about completed historical acts
On February 25, 2021, CSAA made two affirmative representations directly to
Plaintiff:

"We have concluded our investigation of your claim."
CSAA reached its decision "after carefully reviewing the facts and
circumstances of your claim."

The actionable representation is not CSAA's opinion that its insured was
not liable. Plaintiff does not seek to impose liability merely because CSAA
reached the wrong liability conclusion. The actionable representation
concerns what CSAA claimed it had already done: completed an investigation
and carefully reviewed the relevant facts.

Whether an investigation was requested, performed, completed, and
documented is a matter of historical fact. CSAA had exclusive control over
the claim file, activity logs, request history, production indices, and
electronic audit trails showing what its personnel had requested, received,
and reviewed. The statement was therefore capable of being proven
objectively true or false.

CSAA obtained the police report concerning the collision. That report
identified the existence of the 911 recording, body-worn-camera evidence,
computer-aided dispatch records, and associated reference information.
Those materials were not collateral. They contained the insured's
statements concerning contact, eyewitness information, and objective
evidence bearing directly on liability.

Nevertheless, CSAA's claim file records no request for those materials, no
receipt of those materials, and no review of those materials before the
February 25, 2021 closure. CSAA closed the file twenty-one days after the
collision without following the evidentiary sources identified in the
police report it possessed.

CSAA may have performed isolated administrative acts, such as obtaining the
police report or receiving its insured's account. Those isolated acts do
not make its representation true. The representation was that the
investigation had been concluded after careful review of the facts. An
investigation that stops at a report while disregarding the material
evidence expressly identified in that report is not the completed and
careful investigation CSAA represented.

The falsity does not depend on whether CSAA was ultimately correct about
liability. It depends on whether CSAA truthfully described the
investigative acts it had completed when it sent the letter.

*The duty arises independently from the common law*
Plaintiff does not seek to privately enforce the regulatory investigation
requirement. The independent duty arises from Civil Code sections 1709 and
1710. Section 1709 imposes liability on a person who willfully deceives
another with the intent to induce that person to alter his position.
Section 1710, subdivision (1), defines deceit as representing as true a
fact that the speaker knows is untrue.

Even if Plaintiff could not compel CSAA to investigate or settle the
third-party claim, CSAA voluntarily chose to speak. Once CSAA affirmatively
represented that particular acts had been completed, it had the ordinary
common-law duty not to misrepresent those acts. That duty applies
independently of the insurance relationship and would apply to any
commercial speaker making a factual representation for the purpose of
inducing reliance.

The claim therefore does not depend on deriving a private duty from section
790.03. It depends on the generally applicable prohibition against
intentional deceit.

Title 10, California Code of Regulations, section 2695.7(d), requires
insurers to conduct and diligently pursue a thorough, fair, and objective
investigation. Section 2695.3 requires the claim file to document material
events so that the handling of the claim can be reconstructed.

Those provisions remain subject to administrative enforcement
notwithstanding Moradi-Shalal. In the civil deceit action, they are
relevant to the materiality and understood meaning of CSAA's
representation, why the identified recordings were integral to the
investigation, and why Plaintiff reasonably understood "concluded our
investigation" to describe a completed professional process.

Plaintiff does not request civil damages merely because CSAA violated those
regulations. The damages arise because CSAA affirmatively misrepresented
its completed conduct and induced Plaintiff to alter his position.

CSAA exclusively controlled the claim file. Its personnel knew whether they
had requested or reviewed the 911, body-worn-camera, and CAD materials. The
absence of any request, receipt, or review was therefore known to CSAA when
it represented that its investigation had been concluded.

Scienter may also be inferred from the twenty-one-day closure, the reliance
on the insured's account, the failure to pursue the contrary evidence
identified in CSAA's own police report, and CSAA's subsequent adoption and
defense of the completed-investigation representation.

Later litigation statements are not pleaded as separate actionable wrongs.
They are evidentiary admissions relevant to CSAA's knowledge, intent,
institutional adoption, and ratification of the February 25, 2021
representation.

Plaintiff had no access to CSAA's proprietary claim system and no ability
to inspect its internal request history. He reasonably understood CSAA's
statement to mean that the material evidence had been gathered and
evaluated.

In reliance on that representation, Plaintiff refrained from immediately
obtaining the public-safety records himself during the ordinary retention
period. When the truth concerning the empty file was later discovered,
Plaintiff had to retrieve, restore, authenticate, and reconstruct the
missing evidentiary materials at substantially increased cost.

The recoverable injury is the incremental out-of-pocket expense caused by
that reliance and delay, according to proof. It is not the value of the
insurance claim, policy proceeds, collision damages, or damages merely
resulting from CSAA's refusal to settle.

*The later verdict does not adjudicate or cure the deceit*
The personal-injury jury decided whether CSAA's insured was negligent on
the evidence and instructions presented at the 2025 trial. The jury did not
decide:

   1. what CSAA's claim file contained on February 25, 2021;
   2. whether CSAA requested the report-identified recordings;
   3. whether CSAA carefully reviewed the available facts;
   4. whether the completed-investigation representation was false;
   5. whether Plaintiff relied on that representation; or
   6. whether that reliance caused independent retrieval expenses.

The verdict therefore does not create issue preclusion concerning the
investigation or the truth of the denial letter. A later agreement with
CSAA's ultimate liability position does not establish that CSAA truthfully
represented the investigative process it had completed four years earlier.

The core wrong is not that CSAA failed to settle or reached an incorrect
liability conclusion. The core wrong is that CSAA affirmatively represented
that it had completed and carefully performed an investigation while its
own file showed that it had not requested or reviewed the material evidence
identified in the police report it possessed. CSAA made that representation
to induce Plaintiff to accept the file closure and refrain from acting
independently. Plaintiff relied and suffered separate, out-of-pocket
pecuniary injury.

That is traditional common-law deceit under Civil Code sections 1709 and
1710, subdivision (1), expressly preserved by Moradi-Shalal at pages
304-305.
