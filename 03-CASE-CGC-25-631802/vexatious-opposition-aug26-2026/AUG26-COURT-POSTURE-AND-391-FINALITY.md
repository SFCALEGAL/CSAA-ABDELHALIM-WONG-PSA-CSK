## 2. Why the face ruling matches the stay statute (filed Aug 14)

Plaintiff's filed stay notice (Tx `80347745`) quotes Code of Civil Procedure section 916, subdivision (a):

> the perfecting of an appeal stays proceedings in the trial court upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby, including enforcement of the judgment or order, but the trial court may proceed upon any other matter embraced in the action and not affected by the judgment or order.

The same filed notice states the Supreme Court rule in *Varian Medical Systems, Inc. v. Delfino* (2005) 35 Cal.4th 180:

> an appeal from an order granting an anti-SLAPP motion stays further trial-court proceedings on the merits of the claims affected by that order. The stay is automatic upon perfecting the appeal; it does not depend on a separate discretionary stay order. (*Varian*, 35 Cal.4th at 189-191, 198-201.)

And:

> The test is whether the post-appeal proceeding would interfere with the appellate court's jurisdiction, or whether the matter is "embraced in or affected by" the appealed order. (*Varian*, 35 Cal.4th at 189-190; Code Civ. Proc., § 916, subd. (a).)

The filed stay notice then applies that test to the section 391 motion when the motion treats the July 1 anti-SLAPP grant as a final adverse determination, or seeks security or prefiling relief as a downstream consequence of that order. The August 26 face ruling ("Off calendar. Case stayed.") is the trial court's recognition of that stay problem on the record.

## 3. Why section 391(b)(2) is dead on arrival without a final determination

### A. The statute (quoted in the defense moving papers themselves)

Defendant's filed memorandum quotes Code of Civil Procedure section 391, subdivision (b)(2). A person is a "vexatious litigant" under that subdivision if the person is one:

> Who, after a litigation has been finally determined against the person, repeatedly relitigates or attempts to relitigate, in propria persona, either (i) the validity of the determination against the same defendant or defendants as to whom the litigation was finally determined or (ii) the cause of action, claim, controversy, or any of the issues of fact or law, determined or concluded by the final determination against the same defendant or defendants as to whom the litigation was finally determined. (Code Civ. Proc., § 391(b)(2).)

The trigger words are not optional. The statute requires a litigation that "has been **finally determined**," then repeated relitigation of that final determination against the **same defendant**. You do not get a (b)(2) label by pointing at a case that is still on appeal, or by treating an interlocutory anti-SLAPP strike as if every distinct claim had been finally determined on the merits.

### B. Published construction of "finally determined"

*First Western Development Corp. v. Superior Court* (1989) 212 Cal.App.3d 860, 864, states the finality rule for this statute in plain English:

> When, as here, all avenues for direct review have been exhausted, the judgment is final for all purposes.

The same opinion describes section 391(b) as aimed at a person who repeatedly relitigates issues "determined or concluded by the final determination against the same defendant or defendants as to whom the litigation was finally determined." (*First Western*, 212 Cal.App.3d at 868-870.) Later published cases quote the same exhaustion rule: "a judgment is final for all purposes when all avenues for direct review have been exhausted." (See *Childs v. PaineWebber Incorporated* (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 982, 993, citing *First Western*.)

Those authorities were lodged for the August 26 hearing in plaintiff's Request for Judicial Notice of controlling authorities (Item 17 / Digest 5; Tx `80146159` per the Aug 26 readiness register crosswalk). The point for a reader is simple: **pending direct review is not exhaustion**. A pending appeal means the prior determination is not yet "finally determined" for section 391(b)(2).

### C. What plaintiff's filed opposition already said

Plaintiff's filed opposition memorandum (Argument I.C, "Not 'finally determined'") states:

> The personal-injury judgment is on appeal in A173827. (RFJN Ex. H.) Defense attaches the Notice of Appeal while treating the PI judgment as a closed book. Anti-SLAPP orders strike protected-activity allegations; they do not finally determine every institutional-omission theory. (*Baral*, 1 Cal.5th at 393-396; *Bonni*, 11 Cal.5th at 1009-1012.)

And earlier in the same Argument I heading:

> Section 391(b)(2) fails: wrong primary right, wrong defendant, and no final determination of the issues CSAA identifies.

So the dead-on-arrival problem is not a new theory invented after the hearing. It was already on file before August 26: (1) the personal-injury judgment is on appeal in A173827; (2) the July 1 and August 6 anti-SLAPP orders are themselves on appeal as of August 14; (3) an anti-SLAPP strike is not a claim-by-claim final determination of every theory; and (4) section 391(b)(2) still requires the same defendant as to whom the prior litigation was finally determined (the PI judgment ran against Subhi Abdelhalim, not CSAA).

### D. How the three pending appeals lock the predicate

| Proceeding | What is not final | Why it matters to section 391(b)(2) |
|------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------|
| A173827 (CGC-21-594102) | Personal-injury judgment on appeal | Direct review of the PI determination is not exhausted under *First Western* |
| CGC-25-631802 appeal | July 1, 2026 anti-SLAPP order on appeal (Tx `80347004`) | The order the defense leans on for "prevailing" / adverse determination is itself under appellate review; trial-court proceedings embraced by that order are stayed under section 916 / *Varian* |
| CGC-25-631801 appeal | August 6, 2026 anti-SLAPP order on appeal (Tx `80347363`) | Same stay / non-finality problem in the companion case |

A reader who asks "can you brand someone vexatious for filing again when the prior case is not final?" gets the statute's answer: section 391(b)(2) activates only **after** a litigation has been finally determined, then only for repeated relitigation of that final determination against the same defendant. Mid-appeal is the opposite of that statutory gate.
